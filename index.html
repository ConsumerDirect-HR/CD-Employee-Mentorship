You've Got People in Your Corner.

At ConsumerDirect, we put people first — not just for our customers, but for each other. Our mentorship program is how we make that real for you.

What is this program?

We believe no one should have to figure things out alone at work. So we built a two-part mentorship program designed for two different moments in your career here at ConsumerDirect.

🤝

The Buddy System

For your first 60 days. A friendly face, a go-to person, and someone who's already been in your shoes.

🚀

Career Mentorship

For wherever you want to go next. A mentor who helps you grow and build the career you want.

Why does this exist?

Because great companies are built by people who feel supported — not just employed. We want you to feel like you belong here from Day 1, and to know there's always someone willing to invest in where you're headed.

Whether you're brand new, a few years in, or a seasoned pro ready to give back — there's a place for you in this program.

Track 1 · Mandatory for All New Hires

🤝 The Buddy System

Your first 60 days, made easier — by someone who's already been right where you are.

What's a Buddy?

A Buddy is a fellow ConsumerDirect employee — not your manager — assigned to you before your first day. They're your go-to person for questions, introductions, and all the things that don't make it into the employee handbook.

Think of them as your insider guide. They know where things are, who to go to for what, and how life at ConsumerDirect actually works day-to-day.

Your 60-Day Journey Together

✉
Before Day 1
You'll hear from them first
Your Buddy will reach out before you even start — to say hello, ease any first-day nerves, and share a few real tips about what to expect when you walk in the door.
1
Week 1 — The Foundation
They'll be your anchor
They'll meet you at the door on Day 1, show you around in person, grab lunch with you, and check in every single day. Week 1 is intentionally full of connection — because it matters most.
2
Weeks 2–3 — Navigation
They'll help you find your footing
They'll walk you through how things really work — communication norms, the unofficial org chart, who to know, and how to get things done. And they'll keep making introductions so you're building real relationships fast.
3
Weeks 4–6 — Settling In
They become your sounding board
Weekly check-ins, honest conversations, and celebrating your early wins. You're finding your footing — they're right there cheering you on.
✓
Weeks 7–8 — Graduation
You graduate — but they don't disappear
You'll have a final sit-down to celebrate how far you've come in 60 days. The formal program wraps up, but most Buddy relationships keep going naturally long after.

What can I ask my Buddy?

Anything. Seriously. Here are questions people actually bring to their Buddy:

  • "What do people actually wear around here day to day?"
  • "Is it fine if I eat lunch at my desk, or does everyone go together?"
  • "Who do I talk to when I have an IT issue?"
  • "My manager said something and I'm not sure how to read it — can I talk it through?"
  • "I feel like everyone already knows each other. Is that normal at first?"
There are no dumb questions with your Buddy. That's literally what they signed up for.

Want to become a Buddy?

If you've been at ConsumerDirect at least 6 months and you want to be the person who makes someone else's start here genuinely great — we'd love to have you. You'll go through a half-day training first, and our HR team will support you the whole way.

Being a Buddy is a leadership act. It's recognized publicly, noted by your manager, and celebrated regularly. It also just feels really good.

Talk to HR or your manager to raise your hand. We're always building our Buddy pool.

Track 2 · Open Enrollment · Twice Per Year

🚀 Career Mentorship

A real mentor. Goals that are yours. Six months to make meaningful progress on where you want to go next.

What is the Career Mentorship Program?

This is a structured, 6-month program that pairs you with an experienced ConsumerDirect colleague who helps you think through your career, sharpen your goals, and grow in the direction you want to go — whatever that looks like for you.

It's not a class, a checklist, or a performance review. It's a real relationship, built entirely around your goals.

Who is it for?

Anyone who's been here at least 3 months and has something they want to work toward. You don't need to have it all mapped out — "I'm not totally sure what I want next" is a perfectly great place to start.

You might be ready if you're asking yourself: "How do I get to the next level?" "Should I stay on this path or try something different?" "I want to lead someday — where do I even begin?"

How does it work?

✦
Before You Start
You apply and get matched
Fill out a short application about what you want to work on. HR shortlists 2–3 potential mentors for you. You review their profiles and share your preference. Then both of you do a casual 30-minute "get to know you" meeting — no commitment yet. If it feels right, you're matched.
1
Month 1 — Foundation
Build the relationship and set your goals
Your first two sessions are about getting to know each other and setting your goals together. You'll create a simple one-page "Mentorship Agreement" — what you want to achieve, how you'll work together, and what success looks like by Month 6.
2
Months 2–4 — The Real Work
Meet, talk, grow
You meet in person every two weeks — coffee, a walk, lunch, whatever works. You set the agenda each time. You bring the topics and challenges. Your mentor brings perspective, honest feedback, and the right questions to help you think more clearly.
3
Month 3 — Midpoint Check-In
Pause, reflect, adjust
Halfway through, you'll both take a moment to reflect on your progress. If something isn't working — your goals, your approach, even the match — this is the time to adjust. No judgment, no pressure.
4
Months 5–6 — Make It Visible
Turn your growth into momentum
The final stretch is about putting your growth to work — building confidence, making your progress visible at ConsumerDirect, and setting yourself up to keep moving forward after the program wraps.
✓
Month 6 — Graduation
Celebrate how far you've come
Your final session is a real conversation about your journey. Your mentor shares what they genuinely see in you. You share what you're carrying forward. The program ends — but the relationship almost never does.

What will we actually talk about?

That's up to you — you own the agenda. But here are topics that come up a lot:

  • How to get to the next level in your role or career
  • A tough situation with a coworker or manager you're not sure how to handle
  • Whether to go deeper in your current path or try something new
  • How to be more visible without it feeling uncomfortable
  • How to ask for what you want — a raise, a promotion, a new opportunity
  • What kind of leader you want to become
  • Closing a skill gap you know is holding you back

Want to become a Mentor?

You don't have to be the most senior person in the building. You just have to care about someone else's growth, be willing to be honest with them, and show up consistently.

You'll complete a 3-hour orientation before your first cohort and join a monthly Mentor roundtable where you can learn from and lean on other mentors. You're never left figuring it out alone.

Mentors get a lot out of this too. Most say mentoring made them sharper at their own job, gave them fresh perspective, and was one of the most rewarding things they've done here.

Applications open 6 weeks before each cohort. Talk to HR if you're interested.

Your Place in the Program

👤 What's My Role?

Everyone plays a part. Here's what the program looks like from where you're standing.

🎉 Welcome to ConsumerDirect.

Before your first day, HR will assign you a Buddy. You don't need to do anything — they'll reach out to you first. Your only job is to show up, ask questions, and let people help you.

Your responsibilities as a new hire:

  • Be open. Your Buddy is here to help — the more you share, the more they can support you.
  • Ask the questions you're too embarrassed to ask your manager. That's exactly what your Buddy is for.
  • Show up to your check-ins. They're short and they matter.
  • At Week 8, complete your 60-Day Reflection form. Your honest input helps us make this even better for the next person who joins.
You're not expected to have it all figured out. You're expected to be curious, be yourself, and give it your best. That's it.

What about after 60 days?

Once you've been here 3 months, you're eligible to apply for the Career Mentorship Program. It's a great next step if you want to think intentionally about where you're headed at ConsumerDirect. HR will let you know when the next application window opens.

🤝 You're about to make someone's first days here.

Being a Buddy is one of the most human things you can do at work. You're not here to train anyone or solve every problem — you're here to make someone feel like they landed somewhere genuinely good.

Your core responsibilities:

  • Reach out to your new hire before their first day — warmly and personally, not from a template.
  • Meet them at the door on Day 1. Walk them around in person. Have coffee with them.
  • Check in consistently across 60 days — every day in Week 1, then tapering naturally.
  • Introduce them to people. Help them build a real internal network from day one.
  • Share the real stuff — the unwritten norms, the things that took you a while to figure out.
  • Submit your Week 1 Pulse Check and your Exit Survey to HR.

What you're NOT responsible for:

  • Managing their performance — that's their manager's job, not yours.
  • Solving every problem they bring to you — sometimes listening is enough.
  • Being available every hour — reasonable check-ins, not constant connection.
If something comes up that feels beyond your role — a concern about their wellbeing, something about their manager, anything that feels serious — bring it to HR. You never have to handle it alone.

What makes a great Buddy great?

  • They remember what it felt like to be new — and lead with that empathy, always.
  • They're honest. Not harsh — just real. They share actual tips, not just cheerful ones.
  • They ask questions more than they give answers.
  • They celebrate the small wins. Week 1 is hard. Telling someone they're doing well matters more than they let on.

🌱 You own this relationship.

As a mentee, you're in the driver's seat. Your mentor is here to guide, challenge, and support you — but they can only do that as well as you let them in. The more honest you are, the more valuable this becomes.

Your responsibilities as a mentee:

  • Set the agenda before every session and send it to your mentor the day before.
  • Bring real topics — not just status updates. Bring your actual challenges, doubts, and questions.
  • Follow through on what you commit to between sessions. Your mentor will notice, and they'll say something — kindly.
  • Be honest. If something in the relationship isn't working, say so. It won't hurt their feelings. It'll make things better.
  • Complete your Midpoint Self-Assessment and final survey. These keep the program strong for everyone.
The mentees who get the most out of this are the ones who show up prepared, ask for what they actually need, and treat every session like it counts. Because it does.

What if the match doesn't feel right?

It happens, and it's completely okay. At the 60-day mark, you can confidentially let HR know you'd like a different pairing. No explanation required, no awkwardness. We'd rather find you a better fit than have you spend six months in something that isn't working.

🌟 You're not here to have all the answers.

The best mentors aren't the ones who tell people what to do. They're the ones who ask the right questions, give honest feedback, and help someone see themselves and their options more clearly. That's your job.

Your responsibilities as a mentor:

  • Show up to every session present and prepared. Your mentee is counting on this time.
  • Let the mentee own the agenda — resist the urge to turn sessions into your soapbox.
  • Give honest feedback, not just encouragement. Both are acts of genuine care.
  • Hold them accountable, gently. If they said they'd do something and didn't — ask about it.
  • Complete the Midpoint Reflection and final survey. Your perspective shapes how this program evolves.
  • Show up to the monthly Mentor roundtable. The community makes everyone a better mentor.

What you're NOT responsible for:

  • Solving all of their problems — help them think, don't just hand them answers.
  • Being their therapist — if something feels beyond mentorship, encourage them to find the right support.
  • Making their career decisions — you can inform, but they decide.
The best thing you can give someone is your honest perspective. Not the polished, professional version — the real one. That's what they'll carry with them.

You'll be supported the whole way

Before your first cohort, you'll complete a 3-hour in-person orientation. You'll also join a monthly Mentor roundtable — a space to share what's working, talk through tricky situations with fellow mentors, and keep growing in the role. You are never expected to figure this out alone.

💼 Your support changes everything.

You don't run this program — HR does. But your attitude toward it shapes whether your team members actually show up and get something out of it. Managers who champion this program have team members who grow faster and stay longer. It really is that direct.

What we're asking from you:

  • Nominate Buddies. You know who on your team would be great at welcoming someone new. Tell HR — your input directly shapes the matches we make.
  • Encourage your team to apply for Career Mentorship when the window opens. A simple "I think you'd get a lot out of this" from you goes further than anything HR can say.
  • Give Buddies and Mentors the time they need. A coffee with a new hire or a mentee lunch isn't wasted time — it's one of the best investments a team member can make.
  • Acknowledge it publicly. When someone on your team is a Buddy or Mentor, call it out. It signals to everyone that ConsumerDirect means what it says about developing its people.
  • Connect your 1:1s to the program. Ask your team how their mentorship experience is going. It shows you're invested in more than just deliverables.
When your direct reports grow, you grow. When they feel supported, they stay. This program is one of the best tools you have for both.

Can managers be Mentors?

Yes — and we actively encourage it, especially for senior managers and department leads. Mentoring someone outside your direct team is a powerful way to develop your own leadership while investing in ConsumerDirect's next generation of talent. Reach out to HR if you're interested in being matched in the next cohort.
